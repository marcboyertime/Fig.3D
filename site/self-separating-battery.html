An interwoven battery

What if a battery’s two electrodes were woven through each other instead of stacked?

The idea

Every battery keeps its two electrodes apart with a thin separator that lets lithium ions through but blocks electrons. Usually all three are flat layers, stacked or rolled, and each is tens of micrometres thick. Tait et al. weave them through one another instead, around pores about 90 nanometres across.

How they build it

A carbon sponge becomes the negative electrode. A conducting polymer is grown over its inner walls to make the positive electrode, so at first the two touch everywhere. An electrochemical treatment then forms a thin interphase between them, which the authors interpret as the separator. It grows from within; nothing is slotted in.

Reading Figure 1

The colours are the paper’s: grey anode, green separator, blue cathode. Panel a is an ordinary rolled cell, b an earlier ordered 3D design, c this paper’s irregular network. The 3D model keeps those colours. These are proof-of-principle cells: they hold a voltage, but their capacity fades quickly.

The source

William R. T. Tait et al., All-organic self-separating three-dimensionally nanoarchitected electrochemical energy storage devices, arXiv:2604.26222v1, 29 April 2026.

Figure 1 compares a conventional layered cell, an earlier ordered double-gyroid cell and the nonperiodic architecture studied here. Figure 2 sets out fabrication. Figures 5 and 6 report the electrochemical processing and the first measurements.

What “all-organic” means

The electrodes and framework are organic-derived. The SEI can contain organic and inorganic compounds; lithium salts and external metal contacts are also involved. The phrase does not mean that every ingredient is organic.

What is represented

A deterministic, nonperiodic geometry illustrates connected carbon, polymer and interphase domains, drawn in the paper’s viewpoint and colours. It is not reconstructed from the paper’s microscopy. Domain thickness, porosity and surface shape are illustrative; the real coating is heterogeneous. Connectivity is checked on the generating grid.

The magnified walls enlarge the separation to explain function. Moving markers indicate transport roles, not molecular paths, calculated flux or physical time. Highlighted routes prove a connection in this geometry, not a rate of conduction.

The electrochemical steps

The paper processes the coated scaffold against external lithium in liquid electrolyte, then reduces the polymer, charges the device through its own leads and lifts it out of the liquid to cycle. The interphase forming between carbon and polymer is the authors’ proposed interpretation, not a directly imaged growth sequence.

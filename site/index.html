SOURCE & MODEL

From the figure
to the spatial idea

The opening image is the original Figure 1 extracted directly from the paper by Hau et al., Disordered Rocksalts as High-Energy and Earth-Abundant Li-Ion Cathodes, Advanced Materials 37 (2025), 2502766. The native 1500 × 850 pixels are preserved without repainting or AI enhancement. Page headers, captions and screenshot controls are outside this original image. The supplied screenshot remains available below.

We focus on the layered structure’s local 1-TM hop. The zoomed glyph lays back like a page on a desk while its green Li, purple transition metal and dashed vacant sites rise out of it, keeping their identities, into an ideal rocksalt model. A second vacant gate site is revealed in depth: this example uses a divacancy configuration. The three columns in the paper compare different orderings; they are not stages of a phase transformation.

Lithium follows a schematic octahedral → tetrahedral → octahedral route through two triangular faces of oxygen, and each window lights up as lithium crosses it. Bright red spheres are the four oxygens around the tetrahedral site; darker red ones complete the two octahedra on either side. While lithium waits at the tetrahedral site, the one transition metal that shares a face with it turns that face violet. Select Li⁺ to emphasize the six oxygen neighbors of an octahedral endpoint. Atom sizes are symbolic, and the camera briefly widens during the lift to show depth without changing any geometry.

This is an idealized geometric reconstruction, not a crystal structure recovered from the image. The animated geometry is an adaptation for Fig.3D. Motion is illustrative, not a calculated migration trajectory or timescale. No energy barrier, universal channel activity, material performance or diffusion coefficient is calculated. Local relaxation, interactions and collective motion are omitted.
